Table 5.2 - The CLEAR Framework Components
Prompt-engineering framework for EFL students · Ch 5
Component What to Include Why It Matters for L2 Learners Example
C - Context Who you are, your level, your class, your task Allows AI to calibrate register and complexity appropriately "I am a B1 EFL student writing an opinion essay for my university writing class."
L - Level / Length / Limits CEFR level, word count, specific constraints Prevents AI from defaulting to C1/C2 native-speaker output "Write at B1 vocabulary level. Maximum 150 words. Use only present simple and present perfect tenses."
E - Example A sample sentence, your thesis, or a model of what you want Anchors AI output to the student's own voice and level "My thesis is: Social media has more negative than positive effects on students."
A - Action The specific task: generate, improve, explain, ask questions Defines what the AI should produce - prevents vague, comprehensive output "Give me one supporting paragraph with a topic sentence and two pieces of evidence."
R - Role / Restriction The AI's persona; what it must NOT do The most critical component for L2: prevents AI from replacing student thinking "Act as a writing coach. Do not write the paragraph for me. Ask me two questions about my evidence instead."

Table 2.1 - The Lean Tech Map
Mapping tools to stuck points and writing stages · Ch 2
Stuck Point When It Occurs Recommended Tools What the Tool Does What AI Must NOT Do
Engagement Before writing begins; student reluctance, blank page Padlet, Gemini (brainstorming), topic choice menus Lowers anxiety; generates topic options; scaffolds first thinking in either language Generate the essay topic or thesis for the student
Content Knowledge Stage 1–2; student knows what to say in L1 but not in English Perplexity, Gemini (content exploration), Google Scholar Provides topic information at appropriate level; surfaces vocabulary for known concepts Write the argument or provide evidence the student accepts without verification
Language Stages 2–3; vocabulary gaps, collocation errors, sentence errors Gemini (CLEAR prompts), SKELL, Ludwig, Grammarly Generates vocabulary options; verifies collocations; identifies sentence errors Correct sentences for the student without asking the student to think first (see Ask Don't Tell)
Structure Stages 2–3; difficulty organising paragraphs or whole essay Google Slides (clause ordering), essay outliners, Gemini (outline feedback) Scaffolds paragraph structure; provides templates; gives feedback on organisation Write the outline or paragraph plan in the student's place
Revision Stages 4–5; student unsure how to improve their draft Multi-Agent Feedback System (Ch 21), Gemini (targeted revision prompts), peer review via Google Docs Identifies specific weaknesses by dimension; produces prioritised revision targets Rewrite the draft; AI-free Stage 4 is non-negotiable
